# Independent review of the BNR non-equilibrium implementation

Reviewer review of commit `eb74b94` and the outputs regenerated on 2026-10-19 (`outputs/reports/analysis_report.md`, `outputs/tables`, `outputs/figures`). The test suite could not be re-executed by the reviewer because neither the linked computer nor the cloud workspace could reach the package index; the pytest cache records no failure from the implementer's last run, which is evidence of a green run but not an independent execution.

## Decision

**NO-GO** for the stated objective, which is to explain how the sensor data reflect the non-equilibrium state of the system and how that state determines nitrogen conversion efficiency and energy use. The code is a careful and well-tested sensor audit, but it contains no layer that maps sensor signals onto process state, oxygen demand, energy and efficiency, it uses two mutually inconsistent NH4 observation models in the same report, and it omits the alkalinity analysis that the only validated sensor (pH) makes possible and that constrains the conversion more strongly than any other quantity in the dataset.

## Findings

| ID | Severity | Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | P0 | The sensor to process to energy to efficiency chain is absent. Every energy and efficiency output is NaN or NO-GO, efficiency is never defined as a family of metrics, and the report reads as a forensic audit of four probes rather than an analysis of a nitrifying system. | Report status table rows 9 to 16; `pulses.csv` energy columns all NaN; Figures 1, 4 and 8 of the plan, which carry the linkage, were never produced. |
| R2 | P0 | Two incompatible NH4 observation models are combined. M3 rates divide slopes by the pulse response fraction (reading proportional to truth, gain 0.17 to 0.37), M6 fits log R = a + b log C with b ≈ 1.32 and an unidentifiable C0 whose optimizer value (186 mg N/L) is five times the reading (36.5 mg N/L), and free ammonia is computed from the uncorrected reading. Rates, FA, η and oxygen demand therefore rest on different implied true concentrations. | `segments.py` lines 194 to 195 (FA from raw reading) against line 232 (linear gain); `model.py` line 229; report rows 241 to 246. |
| R3 | P1 (P0 for any mechanism claim) | The alkalinity bound is not computed although it is the strongest data-driven constraint available. Nitrification releases 2 mol H+ per mol N, so with pH held between 6.47 and 8.02 and nothing added, cumulative nitrification cannot exceed about Alk0/2: 14, 35 and 70 mg N/L for an initial alkalinity of 2, 5 and 10 meq/L, against 262 mg N/L dosed. Unless the wastewater alkalinity was exceptionally high, most of the dose was never nitrified, true TAN rose far above the electrode reading (consistent with the M6 C0), and FA at pH 7.5 to 7.8 reached about 3 to 8 mg NH3/L at 200 mg N/L TAN rather than the reported 0.2 to 1.2 mg NH3/L. That reverses the mechanistic reading: alkalinity exhaustion and free ammonia inhibition, not biomass decay, become the leading explanation for the falling conversion. | `balance_alkalinity.csv` stops at "not computed"; no alkalinity or carbonate state in M6; reviewer arithmetic with Anthonisen FA and 2 eq per mol N. |
| R4 | P1 | The energy metric is mis-specified. Stirrer electrical power of a laboratory magnetic stirrer neither transfers to plant aeration nor is measured. The transferable quantities are oxygen demand (4.57 g O2/g N for nitrification, 3.43 for nitritation), oxygen supply capacity, their ratio, and aeration-equivalent energy under an explicit field aeration efficiency scenario. The time the system spends stirred but inactive is the efficiency signal the sensors can actually resolve. | `pulses.py` lines 89 to 233; plan Phase 5. |
| R5 | P1 | Process information in pH and relative ORP is not used. Reviewer Theil–Sen slopes on raw data (electrode uncorrected) show faster NH4 decline in ORP-low windows than in adjacent ORP-high windows (−2.16 against −1.16 mg N/L/d after pulse 2; −0.95 against −0.09 mg N/L/d around pulse 3), and a pH rise of about +0.10/d during days 18 to 22.4 when NH4 consumption is near zero, which is the signature of CO2 stripping without acid production. This is the same ORP, pH and NH4 logic used for aeration control in full-scale BNR and is the natural bridge to energy, but the pipeline uses pH only for ordinal irreversibility and treats ORP change points as uninterpreted events. | Reviewer script on `data/*.csv`; `dynamics.csv`; Figure 2. The ORP-low window after pulse 2 overlaps held NH4 output, so the association is preliminary. |
| R6 | P1 | The effort is concentrated on estimators that the data cannot support. pH tie fractions are 0.82 to 0.96, 12 of 187 pH windows are stationary, the Onsager test is interpretable for 0 of 4 pulses, and NH4 sub-stamp times (22 to 450 rows per 14.4 min stamp) are reconstructed by uniform spacing, so fine-scale NH4 patterns are partly synthetic. None of these results connects to rate, energy or efficiency. | Report rows 11, 196 to 226; `timebase.py`. |
| R7 | P1 | The two GO findings carry no information from the data. The 317 kJ/mol coexistence affinity is computed from assumed concentrations, ΔA = RT ln(C+ΔC)/C is a log ratio of readings, and the check σ = rA/T ≥ 0 multiplies a positive scenario affinity by a non-negative modelled rate, so it is true by construction. Non-equilibrium is never quantified from the measured trajectories in a form that links to rates, such as relaxation times or distance from an inactive reference state. | Report rows 9, 10, 265. |
| R8 | P1 | Rate estimates depend on detector choices. The record is cut into 25 sub-segments; slopes of −21.8, +7.97, −8.49 and +5.48 mg NH4+/L/d between days 2.7 and 5.6 are electrode artifacts that remain in the main table; the 2.59 to 8.82 d slope reverses sign against its reference (+0.99 against −3.42) because the window was truncated to 6.65 to 8.53 d; maximum oxygen demand (77.5 against 33 mg O2/L/d) and required kLa (9.4 against 4 d−1) fail their references. | Report rows 82 to 105, 122 to 131. |
| R9 | P1 | The closed 100 mL mass-balance frame is not verified. A stirred open beaker held for 50 days normally loses water by evaporation at an order of millilitres per day unless covered or topped up, which would concentrate chloride, TAN and alkalinity; the conductivity record nevertheless ends at its starting value. Volume, cover and top-up history must be reconciled, together with the dose and vessel identity, before three simultaneous sensor failures are accepted as the explanation, because the implementation prompt required the report to state those failures as gates (prompt line 256), which pre-committed the conclusion. | `config/experiment.toml`; `implementation_prompt.md` lines 241 to 256. |
| R10 | P1 | Time is confounded with every descriptor (Spearman ρ = −1 with pulse time, n = 4), and the model set does not contain the alkalinity or inorganic carbon limitation that R3 identifies, so the selection of "biomass decay" (kd 0.13 d−1) cannot be distinguished from electrode slope decay or substrate-side limitation. | `pulses_associations.csv`; `model_fit.csv`. |
| R11 | P2 | The report is hard to read: 23-column tables, unrounded values (for example 264.8776809451141 mg N/L), SCENARIO labels on most numbers, and no narrative connecting sections. The validation table checks reproduction of preliminary numbers, which is regression evidence, not external validation. | `analysis_report.md`. |
| R12 | P2 | Packaging placeholders: `pyproject.toml` description and the `bnr_non_equilibrium_2026:main` entry point are template leftovers. | `pyproject.toml`. |

## What is sound

Data ingestion, timestamp handling, the pulse-based sensor tests, held-output detection, chronological model splits, profile-likelihood identifiability and the explicit missing-input paths are carefully implemented and should be kept. The problems lie in what the pipeline is asked to conclude, not in its coding hygiene.
